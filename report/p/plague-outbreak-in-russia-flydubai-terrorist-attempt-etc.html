The United States has told France and Germany to release diesel stocks or face an export ban. European refineries have closed 30 facilities between 2009 and 2024, leaving diesel prices at record highs relative to oil prices, which in hindsight was incredibly poor planning. 
EU strategic reserves are minimum commercial holdings rather than government-controlled stocks like the US SPR, and releasing them does not compel energy companies to sell diesel because they function as countercyclical buffers. Governments would need to intervene directly in wholesale prices or the United States would need to remove regulatory incentives such as RINs that encourage US producers to discount barrels into Europe, yet Brussels or Washington are aware that EU reserve barrels have remained undrawn for months due to structural price differences. The same way that regulators releasing the CCyB does not compel a bank to reduce its capital ratio or make additional loans, releasing the strategic reserve does not compel European energy companies to sell diesel, as seen during COVID when many banks did not tap strategic buffers even when told it was ok to do so.
