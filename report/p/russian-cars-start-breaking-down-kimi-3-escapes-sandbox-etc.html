Hello, and thanks for reading! It was a long but productive week at Black Hat. I did two panels, one presentation, two book signings and about 30 meetings. I will do a more thorough writeup on some of the AI stuff, which was all anyone could talk about. 
The most notable thing I learned while I was out there was that out of the seven insurance carriers I spoke to, there were literally zero claims for AI-enabled attacks. Not one. I am under Chatham House Rules, so I can't name them, but they represent about 30% of the total cyber insurance market and are in everything from small and mid markets to enterprise markets. Of course, the Infosec industry isn't a fan of this messaging because it doesn't include nation-states or non-loss-producing breaches. Nevertheless, the financial adversary has thus far been unable to produce any meaningful losses deemed AI-enabled by their DFIR partners. 
There are lots of reasons this may be happening, which I will enumerate in a blog post at some point. All of this jives very closely with the theme of the book The End of Guessing as well, which was nice - and fits in with our general theme. We need to be a lot more data-driven as an industry, and less on feels and vibes, in my humble opinion. Fear, uncertainty, and doubt are not a good strategy. Okay, on with it!
In Russia/Ukraine News, Ukrainian Defense Forces struck the Syzran and Ilsky oil refineries overnight, with fires reported at both facilities. The Syzran refinery in the Samara region has now been hit three times since May, with the latest strike occurring after prior damage on August 4, July 12, and May 21, leaving key processing units such as AVT-6 inoperable. The facility processes nearly 9 million tons of crude oil annually and has remained largely offline through the summer due to repeated strikes and repair cycles that typically require 4-8 weeks. That's a pretty significant outage, and letting the repairs happen actually weakens Russia further, because the repairs cost a lot of money, and during that time the outage still occurs anyway. Smart.
Ukrainian drone operators from the 414th Separate Unmanned Systems Brigade, 20th Unmanned Systems Brigade, 413th Unmanned Systems Regiment struck 12 vessels from Russia’s shadow fleet in the Black and Azov Seas. A Pantsir-S air defense system attempted to intercept the attack but failed to stop the strikes, suggesting the Pantsir system is either not fully stocked in that region or has accuracy issues, as you can see it fly by in the video.
A couple of other vessels were hit near Tuapse as well. So it still appears that the seas are largely unprotected, and the threat of arming the ships with air defenses has not materialized. At least not yet, or at scale.
The Ukrainians also appear to have struck the city of Gelendzhik around the same time as the two tanker strikes.
In other strikes against oil/gas, Ukrainian forces struck the Taman marine terminal near Volna in Krasnodar Krai on July 30, destroying twelve 5,000-ton tanks and causing a fire that covered over 17,000 square meters. The terminal serves as a key hub for transshipment and export of oil, fuel oil, diesel, and LPG while directly supplying fuel to Russian troops. 
Ukrainian drones attacked the Russian-occupied Gvardeyskoye Airfield in Crimea, striking drone launch sites and causing multiple fires confirmed by NASAMS satellite detections. Explosions also occurred in Gelendzhik, where a tanker was hit during the strikes. This airfield was likely a staging point for a lot of the coastal air defense and glide bombing runs against eastern Ukraine.
Russian farmers face bankruptcy risks with many operations on the brink due to record-low grain prices and increased fuel prices that make harvesting unprofitable. Grain is being dumped on the ground rather than collected, and analysts warn that tens of thousands of farms could disappear if conditions persist, leading to reduced production and higher food prices. I am seeing numbers ranging from 1 in 3 to 1 in 5 farmers facing a desperate financial situation. This translates to much higher food prices as well. To some extent, this should be self-correcting, though, because as food prices increase, so too should grain prices. Perhaps this is a short-term shock or government intervention, such as price capping, causing it. I'm not sure.
Wildberries is relocating logistics warehouse operations to Belarus, Kazakhstan, and Uzbekistan after repeated Ukrainian strikes on its Russian facilities. The company seeks to protect inventory from attacks that have disrupted its domestic operations. The idea is that they will move inventory around by truck rather than store it closer to the end user. That's not necessarily a bad idea, as Ukraine has been relatively careful not to let foreign governments drag it into the war. 
Speaking of which, Ukraine agreed to a U.S.-brokered deal to refrain from attacking non-Russian tankers in the Black Sea and Caspian Pipeline Consortium infrastructure unless vessels are under Ukrainian sanctions or carrying Russian cargo. The agreement clarifies the legitimate targets for shippers using the routes. That would allow other shipping to continue unmolested and stabilize the markets that rely on that shipping to some degree.
I'd like to briefly talk about the drone situation, which has decidedly been a contested conversation online. For instance, there is some analysis by the Russians that only 1-2% of drones have been hitting their targets due to Russian interceptions. Of course, the Russians are pretty bad at statistics that look bad. But in return, Russians have been firing ballistic missiles against Kyiv that have virtually zero interceptions. Granted, a lot of that has to do with a lack of interceptors, but not entirely. Unlike the slow-moving counterparts, which can and do get intercepted by a variety of air defenses, ballistic missiles have a far easier time penetrating deeper and hitting something - and maybe even the target! Of course, yes, guidance is another issue. But if those numbers are relatively accurate, the drone swarm requires around 50-100 for every one successful hit against a defended target.  That should give us a sense for how big drone swarms of the future will have to be. Of course, there is no reason to believe Russia's numbers. Why? Because the losses don't quite add up.
For example, Russian authorities reported 42,860 troops lost in July, marking a 2026 record, along with all-war records in destroyed vehicles and fuel trucks totaling 13,998. Losses in air defense systems, artillery, and MLRS also approached records. I think if we are to believe Russia's numbers, we have to squint and consider only the attacks against oil and gas, or attacks against Russian civilian buildings or something, because otherwise, Ukraine would have launched around 5,000,000 drones in a month, and that doesn't sound correct. I would believe hundreds of thousands, but not multi-millions. Not yet, anyway. Granted, I think that is the intention though, so even if Russia is right, this doesn't improve the situation much for Russia.
Trent Telenko spotted a video where he was able to calculate that the Fire Point drones are indeed below 100 feet as they fly, which means that the operating range of interceptors is going to be very short, because they will be flying right above the treetops. That means that you need just that many more interceptors and detection points to handle these drones, even if they are huge and slow-flying.

There has been a slight fall-off in the amount of fuel shortages that have been reported in Russia, and the theory is that some of that initial shock was due to panic buying but the shortages are continuing nonetheless. But it also could be due to another factor as well. Russia has authorized the production and sale of Euro-2 grade through July 2027 which has a number of bad implications. The first is that jet fuel can freeze at -50C instead of -60C. That may not sound like a huge difference, but -50 is a regular low in Siberia, and it can get quite colder. In summertime that's not a huge deal, but winter is only a few months away. 
But worst of all, modern engines designed to Euro-5 standards are experiencing injector, fuel pump, and spark plug failures when using the lower-quality surrogate, which contains sulfur levels exceeding the norm by 50 times. Most modern Chinese engines are not rated for it, so they have already begun to break down. Russian media reports widespread vehicle breakdowns due to refueling with degraded gasoline. That could have a massive negative impact on the population who have fuel that is slowly rotting their cars' injectors and catalytic converters.
