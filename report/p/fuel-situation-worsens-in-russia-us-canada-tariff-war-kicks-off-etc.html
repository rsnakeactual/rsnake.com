In Russia/Ukraine News, the ongoing fires around Russian refinery units continue to erode structural strength by reducing their ability to withstand weight, pressure, and temperature, yet no units have been stopped for proper assessment, leaving operators unaware of the true damage. Real repairs would require shutting down facilities hit by tens or hundreds of kilograms of explosives and replacing damaged units, but Russia is instead halting maintenance to maximize output and substituting quality gasoline with low-grade fuel that damages engines. That's a very good thing for Ukraine but awful for Russia, because it means that many of the working refineries are actually ticking time bombs from a structural perspective. Russia faces a gasoline shortage rather than a diesel shortage because production of gasoline currently falls short of domestic demand. Many gas stations now hold diesel stock only.
Moscow is experiencing fuel shortages along with many other regions, the diesel export ban continues through the end of 2027. This graphic gives you a sense of the problem and also the areas most effected tend to be in the west, nearest the front.
Just to give you an example, a 9-kilometer queue formed on the M-4 Don highway toward Moscow for gasoline. Vehicles occupied the right lane from kilometer 1009 to 1018. Can you imagine waiting in that line for hours and running out of fuel, and now needing a tow to the gas station? What a mess. These longer queues should ebb and flow as people hoard fuel and then actually run out and then hoard again, etc.
Strikes by drones on Russian refineries have reduced their ability to process crude into finished fuels. The destruction of reformers, Poly or Alkyl units gone, leaves refineries making lower volumes of gasoline, diesel, and kerosene, and fuels of progressively lower octane unless additives are used. The cumulative effect of successive strikes has de-rated these facilities. Knocking out the hydrotreaters forces refineries to make higher-sulfur fuels and to reduce their standards to Euro2, while knocking out the cat cracker reduces gasoline output. The primary problem is the loss of complexity in each refinery. To sell all the crude they cannot refine, Russia will have to shut in wells upstream in West Siberia and the Urals, and many of those wells may never come back online, so overall production of crude will also be permanently reduced. A night view of burning oil refinery in Novokuybyshevsk, Samara Oblast shows one of the facilities affected.

Some researchers did some interesting analysis around the reproducibility of vulnerabilities and the multi-run approach. Interestingly, DeepSeek located 17 vulns on a single run, but DeepSeek was given 32 real vulnerabilities. Repeating the identical setup three times and combining outputs raised the total to 28 out of 32. Qwen increased from 19 to 26 and Kimi from 17 to 25 under the same multi-run method.
